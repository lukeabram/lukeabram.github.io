NVIDIA, Blackwell Architecture technology page

NVIDIA’s marketing overview of the Blackwell architecture. It is short and gives few numbers, but it is the one public NVIDIA page that names all of Blackwell’s specialized engines together. Use it to confirm which features NVIDIA treats as headline features, then go to the whitepaper and the measurements for detail.

Read at NVIDIA.

This page covers the RAS Engine, confidential computing with TEE-I/O, the second-generation Transformer Engine, and the Decompression Engine.

The page has no date. The footer reads “Copyright © 2026”. Product pages change without notice, so record the date you read it. It covers base Blackwell, with Blackwell Ultra (B300, GB300) mentioned in passing. Used in sections A, C, D.

Where to look

  • “A New Class of AI Superchip”. 208 billion transistors on TSMC 4NP, with two reticle-limited dies joined by a 10 TB/s chip-to-chip link. The page does not use the name NV-HBI.
  • “Second-Generation Transformer Engine”. Covers micro-tensor scaling, the community-defined microscaling formats, and FP4. The page gives no FP4 throughput figure. Its “2X attention-layer” and “1.5X more AI compute” claims describe Blackwell Ultra, not B200.
  • “Secure AI”. Calls Blackwell “the first TEE-I/O capable GPU in the industry”, with inline protection over NVLink. It claims near-identical throughput to unencrypted modes but gives no numbers. The confidential computing blog post has the measurements.
  • “NVLink and NVLink Switch”. Fifth-generation NVLink scales to 576 GPUs, gives 130 TB/s in an NVL72 domain, and runs at 1.8 TB/s per GPU.
  • “Decompression Engine”. Names LZ4, Snappy, and Deflate, and the 900 GB/s bidirectional link to a Grace CPU (on GB200, not HGX B200). It gives no decompression throughput. For that, see the nvCOMP FAQ and Jarmusch 2026 §V-B.
  • “Reliability, Availability, and Serviceability (RAS) Engine”. A dedicated engine that predicts faults, monitors hardware and software telemetry, and helps localize problems. The page describes the engine only in general terms, with no numbers.
