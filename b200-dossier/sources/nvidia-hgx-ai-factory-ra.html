NVIDIA, HGX AI Factory Enterprise Reference Architecture: Components

The Components chapter of NVIDIA’s Enterprise Reference Architecture for HGX H100, H200, and B200 systems. It lists what goes into a reference 8-GPU node: the HGX B200 baseboard, NVLink and NVSwitch, host CPUs and memory, PCIe links, and the BlueField-3 SuperNICs and DPU. It is the most specific public NVIDIA source for how a B200 sits inside a server.

Components chapter. Other chapters of the same guide cover networking hardware, physical topologies, logical network architecture, and node configurations (appendix).

The page reads “Last updated on Aug 31, 2026”. The “latest” URL can change, so record the date you read it. Used in section C.

Where to look

  • “NVIDIA HGX B200 8-GPU Baseboard” and Table 1. 180 GB of HBM3e and up to 8 TB/s per GPU; 1.44 TB and up to 64 TB/s per 8-GPU node; up to 1 kW per GPU. The 64 TB/s figure is aggregate HBM bandwidth (8 × 8 TB/s), not NVLink.
  • “HGX H100, H200 or B200 8-GPU Systems” and Table 2. Fifth-generation NVLink with fourth-generation NVSwitch: 1,800 GB/s GPU to GPU and 14.4 TB/s aggregate across the board. The table also gives host requirements: two x86 sockets, at least 1.5 TB of system memory, eight PCIe Gen5 x16 links from the baseboard, and one PCIe Gen5 x16 link per NIC or DPU.
  • “Compute (Node East/West) Ethernet Networking” and Table 4. One 400 Gb/s BlueField-3 SuperNIC per GPU (a 1:1 GPU-to-NIC ratio) for scale-out traffic. The bandwidth figures in this section are per-node totals.
  • “Converged (Node North/South) Ethernet Networking” and Table 5. The BlueField-3 DPU used for storage and management traffic.

Its 180 GB per GPU agrees with the Tuning Guide, not the 192 GB in Jarmusch 2026. See Where the sources disagree.
